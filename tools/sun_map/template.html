How this is worked out

  • Buildings: outlines from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors). StuyTown floors are Terrace, Main, then 1–11 or 1–12; Peter Cooper Village is Main then 1–14. Top floors come from the listings' “top floor” tag where there is one.
  • Sun score (0–100), the number to compare by: morning + 0.75 × afternoon + a bonus of half the smaller of the two (up to 1.5 h), so all-day sun ranks highest, mornings beat afternoons, and some of both beats a lot of one. It counts “full-sun hours”: sun straight into the window counts fully, sun skimming along the wall barely counts. October–March count 1.5× because the dark months are when light matters most, and the courtyard trees take sun off floors 1–5, more in summer. 100 = the sunniest apartments in the complex. Rank compares a line with every line on the same floor (top 5% = sunnier than 95% of them); “#1 of 8” is its place in its own building.
  • Hours: AM h and PM h are plain hours of direct sun before and after noon on an average day (the 21st of every month), trees taken off. Dec 21 is the shortest day. NOAA sun positions every 10 minutes; a window is in sun when the sun is in front of it, over 2° up, and no building, its own wings included, is in the way.
  • Which apartment is where: CBRE's 2006 sale brochure has lettered plans for 272–280 and 330 First Ave, 400–410 and 442–450 E 20th, 435–445 E 14th and the Peter Cooper Village typical floor. Buildings of the same design are matched by outline and turned the right way using listing sizes and view tags. For designs with no plan (the Oval ring, 521–525 E 14th, 522–526 E 20th, 605–625 E 14th, 622–628 E 20th, 315–325 Ave C, 310 First), the 8 apartments of a floor are placed round each entrance's core from the outline, two per wing and lettered clockwise as in every plan, and the listings (Oval / river / Empire State views, one- or two-sided layouts) decide where A starts. Those answers are odds, not facts.
  • Confidence: sure the building's own plan, a same-design building confirmed by several listings, or a line you confirmed. likely one direction has 80%+ of the odds. either way two answers about equally likely (often north side or south side); the score is then the odds-weighted average and the range (e.g. 17–83) is worst to best case. unknown nothing to go on.
  • Getting sharper: the watcher saves the market-rate listings every morning, and each rebuild (tools/sun_map/build.py) learns from them. One line you confirm at a viewing or with the leasing office (tools/sun_map/inputs/line_notes.json) fixes every letter in that building.
